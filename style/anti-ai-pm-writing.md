# Anti-AI PM Writing Style

> Patterns to detect and remove from every PM output this framework produces. Every specialist applies this as a final pass.

---

## What this file is for

AI-generated PM writing has recognisable failure modes. This file names them so specialists can catch and remove them before output reaches a human. The patterns below are not stylistic preferences — they're signals of hollow thinking dressed as substance.

---

## Patterns to remove

### Significance inflation
Language that inflates the importance of ordinary decisions.

Examples to eliminate:
- "This represents a pivotal shift in..."
- "At its core, this fundamentally transforms..."
- "A key milestone in our journey toward..."
- "This is a critical step in building..."

Replace with: the actual claim, stated plainly.

### Hollow trade-offs
Acknowledging trade-offs without naming them.

Examples to eliminate:
- "There are trade-offs to consider."
- "This approach has both advantages and disadvantages."
- "Balancing these competing priorities will be key."

Replace with: the specific trade-off, your position on it.

### Comprehensive emptiness
Listing everything without saying anything.

Examples to eliminate:
- "This covers all relevant aspects including X, Y, and Z..."
- "A robust, scalable, and flexible solution..."
- Three-adjective noun phrases anywhere

Replace with: the one thing that matters most, stated directly.

### Challenges-and-future-outlook closers
Every AI document ends the same way. Remove these on sight.

Examples to eliminate:
- "While challenges remain, the future looks bright..."
- "As we continue to iterate and improve..."
- "Looking ahead, there are exciting opportunities..."
- Any closing paragraph that starts with "In conclusion..."

Replace with: open questions, next steps, or nothing.

### Hedge words in assertions
"Should", "may", "might", "could potentially" — forbidden in assertions. Permission decisions are binary (or explicitly conditional). Write accordingly.

Examples to eliminate:
- "Users should be able to..."
- "The system may permit..."
- "This could potentially allow..."

Replace with: "Users can...", "The system allows...", or "Expected: conditional (caveat: X)."

### Copula inflation
"Represents", "stands as", "serves as", "acts as" — inflated ways of saying "is."

Examples to eliminate:
- "This represents a significant improvement..."
- "The schema serves as the foundation for..."

Replace with: "This improves...", "The schema is the foundation for..."

### Generic regulatory citation
Citing a regulation without explaining what it requires in this context.

Examples to eliminate:
- "Complies with FCA regulations."
- "In line with GDPR requirements."

Replace with: the specific rule or obligation and what it means for this use case.

---

## How to apply this

Read the output. Find the patterns. Remove them. Replace each removed phrase with the actual claim, the actual trade-off, the actual requirement.

If you can't replace a removed phrase with something specific, it means the original was filling space. Remove it and don't replace it.
